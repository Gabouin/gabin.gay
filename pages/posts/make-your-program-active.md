# How to get teens to actually post in your Hack Club program's channel

A lot of high quality Hack Club YSWS (You Ship We Ship) programs are facing a real issue in their channel: nobody talks. This shouldn't be taken lightly as message activity creates strong engagement around your program. As I've observed, a 10k users flagship program with 60% fewer messages posted in its channel than a 2k users community-orged program will have a noticeably weaker community, which is regrettable for a flagship. As we all know, Hack Club is a place for teenagers to create cool projects and make friends. That's why a strong community will make your program memorable and, in 10 years, these teens will actually talk about Hack Club as a beautiful place, thanks to YOUR program.

There are a lot of things that can be done to improve the message activity in your program, and so, to improve the community. This blog article will be about me trying to classify them, thanks to my small experience. 

## Be the change you want to see.

If you want people to start talking and make friends in your program's channel, you need to launch this movement yourself. Start being the one talking a lot in the channel, be friendly, start high quality topics and you'll see that a few people will answer, being friendly as well, starting new topics and this will eventually loop, creating more and more activity. Studies say that it takes 66 days to build a habit. However, it will definitely take less time for people to see your program's channel as the cool and active place to be in the HC Slack and this will also give your program a good reputation.

## Run community events!

Show and Tells are events that regularly happen in [Hack Club](https://hackclub.com)'s community. In them, teens get to showcase and talk about one of their projects for a few minutes, while other teens listen to the presentation and get inspired. 

From what I've seen, they are one of the best ways to improve the quality of conversations in the program's channel which they are organized for. Teens talking proudly about the projects they just finished to other teens is the perfect way to bring up good energy, positive reactions and even motivation for those other teens to keep it up with projects. This will start discussions about the projects seen, how did they do it, what for, how to recreate it, and these discussions would last even after the Show and Tell ends. 

### How to run a high quality show and tell

Running a Show and Tell is actually **pretty easy** and can almost be improvised! You just need to announce it beforehand, start a huddle in your channel and just let people come and present their projects! However, if you want your show and tell to actually create engagement, I have a few tips for you that i got to learn from my past experience. 

1. **Don't run a Show and Tell alone!** You'll need at least another person to help you in the background. While you're the host of the Show and Tell and get to animate it, someone has to write down the projects presented, verify if they were actually shipped to your program (it's usually a requirement in Show and Tells), look for the project's repo to link in the chat, time their presentation (if you set a limit time) and more!
2. **Don’t let your attendees get bored!** For most of the Show, people will be presenting their projects and answering questions about them, but there can be some idle time, for example while you’re waiting for someone to arrive before they start their presentation! When that happens, the best thing to do is keep talking about the previous project and ask the person who built it more technical questions, like how exactly they did X or Y. This lets them go more in depth about what they spent hours making, and it’s usually not boring: they’ll be passionate about it! Another cool thing you can do is simply talk about anything. Even the simplest questions can spark great conversations when you’re in a huddle with 10-20 other teens!
3. **Don't imrpovise your Show and Tell!** I know it’s a tad contradictory with what I said before, but my best advice is to actually work on your event before launching it, instead of just announcing it, jumping into the huddle and praying people will start doing everything by themselves. That includes checking with your friend, your Show and Tell co-org, about what you need them to do to have a smoothly running event. I’d also advise you to create 2 forms beforehand. The first one is for people to write down the project they’re going to present when they arrive, so you have an actual list of projects to call out and know how many people are still left to be called. The second form is for organizing a vote, if you want to! People would get access to it once every project has been presented, so they can choose the one they liked best!
4. **Give people a motivation to come!** We all wish people would come to our events for fun and fun only, but sadly, that won’t be enough most of the time. Of course they want to come and have a great time at your event, but one thing you can do to get way more engagement is offer prizes! For example, this could be a sticker or a small amount of your YSWS currency for everyone who presents. What works best, in my opinion, is giving a bigger prize to the winner of the final vote. This way, everyone will give it their all during their presentation to show that their project is THE best, so they can win the prize. And even if they leave your event without any prize at all, they’ll still be proud of their presentation in front of several other teens! 

I’m sure there’s a ton of other advice I could give you to help you run the perfect Show and Tell, but in my opinion, these 4 are some of the most important! Hit me up with a DM (@Gabin on Slack) if you need more in-depth advice or have any specific questions!
