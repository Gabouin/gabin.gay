# How to get teens to actually post in your Hack Club program's channel. 

A lot of high quality Hack Club YSWS (You Ship We Ship) programs are facing a real issue in their channel: nobody talks. This shouldn't be taken lightly as message activity creates strong engagement around your program. As I've observed, a 10k users flagship program with 60% fewer messages posted in its channel than a 2k users community-orged program will have a fairly weaker community, which is regrettable for a flagship. As we all know, Hack Club is a place for teenagers to create cool projects and make friends, that's why a strong community will make your program memorable and, in 10 years, these teens will actualy talk about Hack Club as a beautiful place, thanks to YOUR program.

There are a lot of things that can be done to improve the message activity in your program, and so, to improve the community. This blog article will be about me trying to classify them, thanks to my experience. 

## Be the change you want to see.

If you want people to start talking and make friends in your program's channel, you need to launch this movement yourself. Start being the one talking a lot in the channel, be friendly, start high quality topics and you'll see that a few people will answer, being friendly as well, starting new topics and this will eventually loop, creating more and more activity. Studies say that it takes 66 days to build an habit. However, it will definitely take less time for people to see your program's channel as the cool and active place to be in the HC Slack and this will also give your program a good reputation.

### Run community events!

Shows & Tells are events that regularly happen in [Hack Club](https://hackclub.com)'s community. In them, teens get to showcase and talk about one of their projects for a few minutes, while other teens listen to the presentation and get inspired. 

From what I've seen, they are one of the best ways to improve the quality of conversations in the program's channel which they are organized for. Teens talking proudly about the projects they just finished to other teens is the perfect way to bring up good energy, positive reactions and even motivation for those other teens to keep it up with projects. This will start discussions about the projects seen, how did they do it, what for, how to recreate it, and these discussions would last even after the Show & Tell ends.
